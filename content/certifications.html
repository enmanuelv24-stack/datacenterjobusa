Data center certifications that actually move your pay

Most technician postings don't require a certification. The ones that help are the ones that prove you can work safely around power and keep the floor running. Here's how to spend your money and study time.

Start with what the job type needs

Data center work splits into two families, and they want different proof.

IT side (data center technician, DCO tech, server and network install). You rack, cable, swap parts and work tickets. Employers look for hardware and networking basics.

Facilities side (critical facilities, engineering operations, controls). You keep power and cooling running: UPS, switchgear, generators, chillers, CRAHs. Employers look for electrical or mechanical trade experience and safety training.

Check our salary comparison: across the employers we track, facilities roles post higher pay ranges than IT-side technician roles. If you can move toward the facilities side, the training below is worth more.

IT side

  • CompTIA A+. The most common "preferred" item on entry-level technician postings. Two exams (Core 1 and Core 2). CompTIA lists each exam at $274 after its June 2026 price change, so $548 to certify. Worth it if you have no IT background; skip it if you already have hardware experience you can show.
  • CompTIA Network+. $399. Useful for network install and deployment roles. A good second step after A+.
  • CompTIA Security+. $439. Mostly needed for cleared or government-site roles, where it can be a requirement rather than a nice-to-have.
  • Fiber and structured cabling training (for example BICSI or vendor fiber courses). Install and deployment teams value hands-on fiber termination and testing skills.

Facilities side

  • NFPA 70E electrical safety training. Arc-flash and electrical safety. Many facilities postings mention it, and some employers provide it after hire.
  • OSHA 10 or OSHA 30. Cheap, short and recognized on construction and commissioning sites.
  • Trade license or apprenticeship (journeyman electrician, HVAC, EPA 608 for refrigerants). For critical facilities roles this matters more than any data center certificate.
  • Data center-specific programs (for example CDCP from EPI, or Uptime Institute operations courses). Good for understanding redundancy, tiers and how a site is run. They help most when you already work in the industry and want to move up; they rarely get you in the door alone.

Before you pay for anything

  • Read five postings for the exact job you want and count which certifications show up as required.
  • Ask your employer. Many data center operators pay for certifications and safety training.
  • Veterans: several employers run SkillBridge and military hiring programs for technician roles. Search "SkillBridge" on our jobs page.
