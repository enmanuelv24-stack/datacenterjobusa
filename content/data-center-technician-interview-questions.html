Data center technician interview questions, with answers that work

Most technician interviews have two halves: can you do the work safely, and how do you act when something breaks at 3 a.m. Here are the questions that come up again and again, what the interviewer is really checking, and how to answer.

How the interview usually goes

Expect a short recruiter screen (pay, shift, location, start date), then one or more technical and behavioral rounds with the site team. Large operators often add an online assessment first. For contractor and subcontractor roles, the "interview" can be a 15-minute phone call with a foreman about your tools, your travel availability and when you can start.

Before any call, know three numbers: the pay you want, the shift you can work, and the date you can start. Recruiters ask all three in the first five minutes.

Technical questions (IT side)

  • "Walk me through replacing a failed drive / DIMM / PSU in a server." They want order and safety: confirm the ticket and the exact asset, check the server is drained or hot-swap is allowed, ESD strap, swap, verify the part shows healthy, update the ticket, return the failed part with its label.
  • "What's the difference between single-mode and multimode fiber?" Single-mode has a tiny core and goes long distances (often yellow jacket); multimode has a larger core for shorter runs (often aqua). Mention you inspect and clean every connector before you plug it in.
  • "How do you troubleshoot a link that won't come up?" Layer one first: correct port, correct cable type, clean and inspect the ends, check light levels with a power meter, swap the optic or patch cord, then escalate with what you already ruled out.
  • "What is a rack elevation / what does U mean?" One rack unit (1U) is 1.75 inches; elevations show what is mounted at each U so installs match the plan.
  • "Have you used a ticketing system?" Name the ones you used (ServiceNow, Jira, internal tools) and describe how you close a ticket so the next shift understands it.

Technical questions (facilities side)

  • "Explain how a UPS keeps the load up during a utility outage." Batteries carry the load through the gap until the generators start and the transfer switch moves the load; then the UPS recharges.
  • "What is lockout/tagout and when do you use it?" Any time you work on equipment that could be energized. Isolate, lock, tag, verify zero energy, and only the person who placed the lock removes it.
  • "What does N+1 or 2N mean?" Redundancy: N+1 has one spare unit beyond what the load needs; 2N has a fully duplicated system.
  • "A CRAH alarm goes off for high supply temperature. What do you do?" Check the alarm on the BMS, look at the unit and its neighbors, confirm airflow and chilled water, follow the site's escalation procedure (MOP/EOP), and log everything.
  • "Have you worked from a MOP or SOP?" Say yes only if true, and describe following steps exactly and stopping when something doesn't match.

Behavioral questions

Answer with a short story: the situation, what you did, and the result. Two minutes, not ten.

  • "Tell me about a time you made a mistake at work." Pick a real, small one, and spend most of the answer on how you caught it and what you changed.
  • "Tell me about a time you had to follow a procedure you disagreed with." Show you followed it, raised the concern through the right channel, and why that matters around live equipment.
  • "Describe working under pressure on a deadline." Installs, outages, turnovers on a build: be specific about the numbers (racks, cables, hours).
  • "How do you hand off to the next shift?" Clear notes, open tickets updated, anything risky said out loud.

What the big operators tend to focus on

Amazon (AWS) interviews are known for behavioral questions built around its published Leadership Principles, so prepare several short stories about ownership, digging into a problem, and delivering under pressure. Google and Microsoft interviews also mix hardware and networking fundamentals with structured behavioral questions. In every case, safety and following procedure matter as much as speed. Check each company's own career site for the current process; it changes.

Questions you should ask them

  • What is the shift pattern, and how often does it rotate?
  • Is overtime paid after 40 hours or after 8, and how much is typical?
  • What does the first 90 days of training look like?
  • How do techs move from Level I to Level II here?
  • For travel roles: per diem per day, is housing paid, and how long is the rotation?
